# Capability History

Dated entries that `docs/CAPABILITY_MATRIX.md` carried under "Recent updates" until 2026-10-28. It is a historical record and is not added to: a capability change updates its matrix row, its details section and `docs/releases/unreleased.md`.

- 2026-10-19: The runtime-gateway MQTT clients check what the broker answered to each SUBSCRIBE, as the evidence routes and the MQTT-family sensor adapters already did, through the one shared check in `ori/utils/mqtt_suback.py`. Before, the gateway export responder, the gateway heartbeat subscriber and the firmware telemetry subscriber took a sent SUBSCRIBE as a subscription, so a topic the broker refused read as a working link that never received anything. Their session now counts as up only once SUBACK grants a QoS of 0 to 2. A refusal or an answer with no code is logged with the topic and the broker's code, and a SUBSCRIBE that could not be sent with the topic and paho's own error. The session then ends and reconnects under the shared back-off, which keeps growing because a refused session never counts as connected, so a permission corrected on the broker is picked up without a restart. The firmware telemetry subscriber's reconnect hook runs on a grant, not on CONNACK. The gateway reasoning client publishes a request only after its response topic is granted. A refusal fails that request at once, naming the topic and code, instead of waiting out the request timeout. The limit stated for the sensor adapters on 2026-10-12 applies here too: mosquitto's `acl_file` grants the subscription and withholds delivery, which no SUBACK check can see. Host-tested against stand-in clients and paho's own reason codes. Driven against mosquitto 2.1.2 with the dynamic-security plugin through the real paho client, MQTT v3.1.1 (SUBACK `0x80`), with the heartbeat subscriber, export responder and reasoning client, and on paho's callback API v1 and v2 and MQTT v5 (SUBACK `0x87`) through the shared session. The reasoning request failed after one second instead of its 20-second timeout. The heartbeat subscriber was refused three times with a growing back-off; once the broker granted the topic, it subscribed on the next attempt without a restart. The firmware telemetry subscriber was not driven against the broker.
- 2026-10-14: Inbound authority artifacts are received under `evidence-exchange/v3`. A verified artifact that restates a record the runtime already holds is decided against it: a custody acknowledgement agrees on its `custody_at_ms` under either custody generation, keeps the first `key_id` and supplies only a missing record time; a receipt agrees on coverage, so held sequences keep their first time and key and only unreceipted ones are applied; an epoch confirmation agrees on `confirmed_at_ms`, `actor` and `key_id`, and neither answer changes the confirmation record, the active-epoch projection or the obligation. A disagreeing restatement is refused `restatement_conflict` and writes nothing. A confirmation's key is checked against the registration sealed for its epoch, so an earlier epoch sealed under an earlier key is confirmable. Every record one artifact changes is written in one transaction. A store fault (I/O, lock, full, corrupt, permissions), including a sealed chain row digest, envelope digest, registration key or artifact the store cannot give back intact, rolls back, leaves the artifact undecided with no reason and no ingest acknowledgement, releases the broker message, and raises `evidence.application_storage_fault`, which degrades health, gates nothing and clears on the next applied artifact; a refusal whose record cannot be written is still answered. Every evidence transaction, in the chain and the ledger alike, goes through one helper: a rollback that itself fails, or a transaction found already open while none of the store's is live, discards the connection and reopens it, a transaction opened inside a live one is refused as a defect without touching the outer, the error that ended the transaction is the one raised, and the reopen opens the existing file, at the absolute path resolved when the store was first opened, read-write, so a missing database is a store fault rather than a new empty file. Both refusal readers, the retained list and the health summary, read a retained reason outside the vocabulary as a store fault, so health never publishes one. Every held value a decision reads is checked on read, and a bad one is a store fault, never a refusal blaming the artifact and never a rewrite of the first record: custody and receipt state against their vocabularies, times inside the integer zone, custody and authority key ids against their shapes, a recorded confirmation against this device and its registration key, and a sealed artifact's device and epoch as non-empty text. An envelope digest is held to the SHA-256 of the stored envelope, and its chain row digest, device, epoch and sequence to what that envelope names. A state is held to its own fields, so a custody or receipt recorded as none that still carries one is damage, not "not held". Every registration obligation is held to the sealed registration it carries and to its own closing. A confirmation missing while its obligation reads confirmed is damage; a projection an earlier release wrote with no record is not, and stays confirmable. A registration's courier copy is held to its obligation, and an obligation's suspension to the integer zone. A gap in the gapless sealed sequence is damage, not "never sealed". Stops are held to their scope and disposition, and sealed artifact types to their vocabulary. The runtime-gateway envelope verifier raises only an authentication error for anything a sender can put on the wire, including a signature of another shape, a lone surrogate, a non-finite number or nesting past the recursion limit. This covers the inbound evidence route and every other route that verifies gateway envelopes. A courier's acknowledgement time orders its answer only when it is the authenticated signing time, inside the integer zone and no further ahead of receipt than the configured clock skew; otherwise the receive time does. A gateway reasoning response is held to the contract's JSON types, with counts as non-negative integers and confidence finite, and with gateway auth off its body must name this device; anything else is refused as a reasoning error at parse. Gateway heartbeat freshness is the receive time, so a gateway clock running ahead cannot hold a stopped gateway reachable. The inbound route also bounds its own admission, so a message it cannot place is refused by the transport and released at the broker, never left as a redelivery the replay check refuses. The inbound route never raises: any other failure inside ingest is logged and left undecided. An acknowledgement says `applied` with an empty reason or `refused` with a reason from the closed vocabulary, and an ingest outcome that could say anything else cannot be built; the acknowledgement builder checks it again and sends nothing rather than an unrecognised reason. The signal is runtime-health/v4's carried in the v3 snapshot, so emitting it, and `restatement_conflict`, diverges from runtime-health/v3 until v4 is adopted, and the heartbeat names no token for it. `restatement_conflict` retires at a gateway only once it accepts that reason, so this ships beside such a gateway. Disposition cases are driven through the ingest service with a test verifier: no disposition verifier ships and no inbound route carries one. The receiver-state corpus `restated-records-v3`, the carriage retirement corpus and the runtime-health/v4 corpus are vendored at ori-specs `2154b1c`. Host-tested only.
- 2026-10-13: Every reading says what its `timestamp` is, under `gateway-api/v2` Producer Time. The runtime decides `producer_time` at ingest from a closed table keyed by the path and the member it took the time from (`ori/hal/producer_paths.py`), stores it on every history row, and carries it on every `sensor_history` export row and on the reading and history points of a Tier 3 request. A read the runtime requests (I2C, serial and Modbus, psutil, SMART, OPC UA, Growatt, and the HTTP and CoAP pollers, whose cache keeps each poll's basis) is `reported`; a pushed value (generic MQTT, MQTT perception, Victron, LoRaWAN, Zigbee, a firmware device) is `not_reported` and timed by its arrival. LoRaWAN, Zigbee and MQTT perception no longer take a payload time, such as a network server's `received_at`, a coordinator's `last_seen` or a perception payload's `timestamp_ms`, into `timestamp`, since no protocol the runtime reads declares one a measurement; the time is carried in the reading's `metadata.payload_time`, which a payload cannot supply itself. The Tier C approval message's Measured line claims a time only for a `reported` reading, and otherwise reads `not reported by device` or `not stated`; a Tier 3 request sends a reading's stored `timestamp` as it is, never the runtime's clock in its place. History stored before this release is kept as stored and reads `unknown`. Raw history takes the basis as a column, and this release compacts into a new set of tables keyed by basis, so compaction and the bucketed export keep each basis in its own row, in the order `reported`, `not_reported`, `unknown`. The compacted tables an earlier release writes keep their shape, still age and read `unknown`, so a release the installer rolls back to keeps recording and compacting on the same store, and is held to it by a test that runs the v2.5.0 statements against a store this release has used. A raw `not_reported` row, or a history point, without a usable receipt is exported as `unknown`. Both action-log readers, `state action-log` and the gateway `action_log` export, carry `record_kind`, `record_key` (null where none was assigned) and `contributed_to`, read by one rule so that every row passes the contract's checks: a joined row is a `contributor` only with a valid link and no execution, otherwise `contributor_legacy`, and any other row is a `dispatch`. Every open rewrites a stored row the readers read as another kind, which only a table whose columns were added can hold, using the readers' own rule (a link or key that is not text, or an execution that is not the integer 0, never makes a contributor), so attestation, the attestation summary and both readers agree on it, and such a row is never attested. The contract's export-rows corpus is vendored, its row verdicts are reached by the suite's checker, its buckets are reproduced through the export responder, and every row the runtime emits is held to the checks. Not yet: the skill prompt's `{time}` and the HTTP telemetry export still present a firmware reading's arrival as its time, pending their contracts, and `action_log` still pages by count. Host-tested only.
- 2026-10-13: The health snapshot's `status` is the most severe condition that holds: each condition contributes a severity and one fold sets `status` and `critical`, so a later degraded condition no longer overwrites a lost decision record. `critical` is always present and equals `status == "critical"`; the firmware liveness split follows runtime-health/v4 (a stopped loop, a stale tick, a loop that has run a whole window without a successful tick and an expired device are critical, over-capacity and failing ticks degraded, and an orderly stop holds none). The node heartbeat refuses to publish `healthy` for a snapshot it cannot read or map, including a provider that raises, and publishes a local `critical` as `degraded` until a gateway claims the gateway-api/v2 heartbeat rules, as that contract's migration requires. Health reports `evidence.awaiting_receipt_count` and `oldest_awaiting_receipt_age_ms` from the delivery ledger, aged by the greater of the courier's and the runtime's clocks; the runtime's first record of custody is final, so a redelivered acknowledgement cannot make an envelope look newer. runtime-health/v4 defines the age from the courier's time alone, so this is a recorded deviation pending a specs amendment. Host-tested through the real snapshot builder, the heartbeat publisher, the delivery ledger and the inbound evidence route; the snapshot is still runtime-health/v3 in shape, and v4 is not adopted.
- 2026-10-12: An MQTT-family sensor adapter checks what the broker answered to each SUBSCRIBE. A broker that refuses a subscription at SUBSCRIBE, as mosquitto's dynamic-security plugin does, answers with a refusal (SUBACK `0x80` and above) and keeps the connection up, and the adapters took that as subscribed: the link read as up, the breaker stayed closed and reads said no data was cached yet, indefinitely. Any answer other than a granted QoS of 0 to 2, including one with no code or one that cannot be read, now keeps the link down, with the topic and the broker's code named, and the client is closed. The check sits in the shared base, so it covers the generic MQTT, MQTT perception, LoRaWAN, Zigbee and Victron adapters, and a Victron sensor's link stays down if any one of its topics is refused. At start and on a reconnect alike, the refusal is logged at every attempt and retried under the same jittered back-off with no terminal state; a sensor refused at start reports unconnected, with health degraded, until its first accepted reading, so a permission corrected on the broker is picked up without a restart, and until then reads refuse as for a link that is down, without naming the refusal. One limit stands: mosquitto's `acl_file`, which `docs/MQTT_SECURITY.md` tells operators to use, grants the subscription (`Granted QoS 0` on v3.1.1 and v5) and withholds delivery instead, which this check cannot see, so a sensor denied that way still reads as up and silent (#855). Host-tested against a stand-in client and paho's own reason codes; driven in review against mosquitto 2.1.2 through the real client, where the dynamic-security plugin answers `0x80` on v3.1.1 and `0x87` on v5.

- 2026-10-09: Firmware manifest registration applies `firmware-telemetry/v2`'s device-mode rules. `device_mode` must be one of `sensor_node`, `bridge_node`, `actuator_node` and `mixed`, and must be the mode the manifest's channels and actions determine, or registration is refused with `invalid_device_mode`; a manifest with no channel is refused with `no_channels`. - 2026-10-08: Which signatures an admitted Ed25519 key accepts is now a contract, `ed25519-verification/v1`: cofactorless verification with strict decoding, S below L, R a canonical point encoding, and the encoding of [S]B - [k]A equal to R byte for byte. Its corpus is vendored under `tests/vectors/ed25519_verification` and drift-checked with the other ori-specs sets. The runtime's verifier, OpenSSL behind `admit_public_key`, reaches all 295 of its verdicts, and so does the Android runtime's, dalek's `verify` behind its own admission; the corpus is driven through both, and each fails a named case when admission is skipped or dalek's `verify_strict` is used. `firmware-commands/v2` and `firmware-telemetry/v2` now cite both contracts. No verdict changed: the runtime already verified this way. The corpus publishes private seeds, the three RFC 8032 test seeds and its signer's, so their keys join the published-test-key refusal.
- 2026-10-07: An admitted firmware fault event is durable. Its `firmware_fault_events` row commits in the transaction that advances the device's freshness mark, so a failed write leaves neither and the signed fault can be redelivered. Rows are keyed by `(device_id, key_epoch_id, boot_id, seq)`, so a re-keyed device's fault that reuses an earlier key's `(boot_id, seq)` is recorded rather than dropped; existing stores are rebuilt at runtime start with every row and id kept.

The refusal is recorded in `firmware_fault_events` and never as an execution, and no command is reissued automatically. The contract's corpus is vendored under `tests/vectors/firmware_telemetry` and drift-checked with the other ori-specs sets.
- 2026-10-07: An MQTT-family sensor reads one concrete topic, and a wildcard is refused. A sensor `topic` (generic MQTT, MQTT perception, LoRaWAN, Zigbee) or a Victron `portal_id` containing `+` or `#` is refused at config load, naming the sensor and the key, and each adapter refuses it again before dialling the broker. Such a sensor used to load and subscribe and never read: a value is cached under the topic of the message that carried it and read under the configured one. No rule maps one sensor to several topics; a deployment that wants several declares one sensor per topic. Any `+` or `#` is refused, including one that is not a whole topic level, since MQTT allows neither in a published topic. Host-tested only.
- 2026-10-06: The shared Ed25519 admission also refuses a key of mixed order, under clause `mixed_order` of the amended `ed25519-key-admission/v1`. Such a key is a prime-order key shifted by a point of small order, and the unshifted key's private scalar signs under it, so a check that knows a key by its bytes (a published test key, an anchor collision, a rotated-away device key) was passed by shifting it. Python, the Android runtime's Rust admission (curve25519-dalek's torsion check) and the host evidence harness apply it together and are held to the vendored corpus, which adds a seed-derived shifted key and a signature its seed makes under it. The authority key registry loader now refuses the ten mixed-order keys `evidence-exchange/v2` admits, as hardening ahead of that contract's successor. No key RFC 8032 derives is refused. A key's negation (bit 255 flipped) is admitted and signed by the same holder, so every check that recognises a key compares its identity, the encoding with bit 255 cleared: the published test keys are refused under both signs, anchor collision and the anchor-rotation duplicate check compare identities, and re-provisioning refuses a key whose identity matches the current or any earlier key of the device. The authority key registry's one-purpose-per-key rule and the commissioning binding and profile verifiers' collision checks compare identities too. A key related by any other known multiple is not checked, and one related by an unknown multiple cannot be recognised from public material. The check is a full scalar multiplication in pure Python, cached for 1,024 distinct keys: a runtime admitting more distinct keys than that in rotation pays it on each admission, about 2 ms per key on a development host and unmeasured on a Pi. Admission does not settle which signatures a verifier accepts under an admitted key; that is open in ori-specs#288.

- 2026-10-05: A request that joins an act another dispatch holds is recorded as a contributor, not an act. Execution coalesces; authority never does. The contributor's row has `record_kind = 'contributor'`, `executed = 0`, no approval, no authority snapshot and no attestation, and `contributed_to` names the `record_key` the holder reserved before acting, and its row is written after the holder's, so a holder still deciding or still driving is never reported missing; `contributed_to` names the intended holder, not proof its row was written, and a contributor whose holder row never appeared is counted in the attestation summary (indexed on `record_key`) and logged. The joiner returns without waiting on the holder's outcome, so one joined to an uncertain command no longer waits until it resolves, no autonomous override entry is written for it, and a Tier B post-action trigger that joined is not reported as a failed act. Only a `dispatch` row is selected for attestation; the store refuses a contributor whose fields claim execution, approval, authority or attestation, a fresh table holds the same shape in a CHECK, a contributor cannot be marked attested, and a non-dispatch row found pending is refused at reconciliation. Joined rows from the previous release become `contributor_legacy` with no link invented, on every open so a rolled-back release's rows are reclassified on roll-forward, and a legacy row left awaiting attestation is settled refused as history; the writer refuses a dispatch row that claims `coalesced`, while a rolled-back release may still write one, reclassified on the next open.
- 2026-10-05: A host-state approval that fails after its act is recorded still records its decision. A cancellation after the act does the same, and the action row records the act that ran rather than a blank result.
- 2026-10-05: The firmware telemetry verifier holds a device to `firmware-telemetry/v1` where it accepted more. A signed manifest carries the contract's root fields and `deployment_maintenance_limit_ms` alone, each action exactly `action`, `channel` and `authority`, each interlock an object of exactly `name`, `channel` and `action`, both security flags booleans and the limit a positive integer; anything else is refused `invalid_envelope`. `device_uptime_ms` going back within one `boot_id` is refused `uptime_regression` for telemetry and fault events alike, from an uptime stored with the freshness mark and checked again in the store's atomic advance; equal uptime is accepted, a rising `boot_id` resets it, an uptime is compared only against the exact mark it was stored with (key epoch, boot and seq), so a mark a release without the columns moved after a rollback, by a reboot or a key rotation, refuses nothing; a message that loses the atomic advance is refused under the reason the new mark gives (`boot_rollback`, `uptime_regression`, `sequence_replay`, `device_revoked` or not approved), and a row written before the upgrade is seeded by its first message. `v` must be the integer 1 on the manifest, envelope and fault. - 2026-10-05: A courier's answer on a sealed envelope has its own column and survives the publish it answers. `courier_answer` holds the courier's standing answer (`queued`, `queue_full`, `refused`) and `last_failure` the transport outcome of the last publish, so neither erases the other in either order. Each answer is applied in one ledger transaction and only if it is newer than the one standing, by the courier's authenticated signing time, which the shared gateway verifier now accepts only as a JSON integer so the verified and the ordering time cannot differ (the acknowledgement's own time where envelopes are unauthenticated), so a delayed older answer never moves it back and concurrent refusals of one envelope open one refusal episode; a stored answer outside the closed set is refused by a CHECK on a new ledger and, on an upgraded one, fails closed with a CRITICAL at open; an episode ends only when the courier answers otherwise, not on a republish or a broker outage, and an answer a previous release recorded in `last_failure` is carried over on every open with its ordering time, which never moves back, taking the later of the standing answer's time and the row's last attempt. A rollback is not lossless: the previous release does not record `queued`, so a `queued` answer received while rolled back cannot end a refusal episode, and an answer it overwrote in `last_failure` before the roll-forward is not carried over.

- 2026-10-05: A cached sensor no longer serves a value the source has moved past, and a broker that drops is reconnected. In every MQTT-family adapter, HTTP and CoAP, a payload refused for a sensor withdraws that sensor's cached value, so reads refuse and name the refusal until an accepted payload arrives; a refusal on another topic leaves it served. A source that has not published, or whose last payload was refused, is not counted by the circuit breaker, and a fresh value closes an open breaker, so a Tier D source that started late is observed within one poll; HTTP and CoAP probe an open breaker every poll interval and close it on the first answer. An MQTT-family adapter keeps one listener that reconnects with jittered back-off up to 60 s, resubscribes, and refuses reads from the drop until a value arrives on the new connection; this was driven against a real mosquitto broker stopped and restarted. At start, a broker that cannot be reached, refuses the connection (bad credentials included) or a subscription, fails the TLS handshake, or gives no answer within the timeout is retried; anything else fails the connect. The retry uses the same back-off instead of leaving the sensor skipped until a restart; the sensor reports unconnected, its reads refused, until its first accepted reading, and if a notice was sent at start, the operator is told when it is read; this is tested against a faked client, not a real broker. A connection that drops within 30 s of connecting does not reset the back-off, and a connect the broker accepts but never answers is closed rather than left to paho's keepalive. A retained message is never a reading on any adapter: the on-change adapters (Victron, LoRaWAN, Zigbee) have no silence bound, so a replay given a fresh arrival would be served as current indefinitely, and they now stay without a value, reads refused, until a live publication arrives, which can be hours. One limit stands: a broker that hangs without closing the connection is noticed only after twice the keepalive. HTTP treats a 4xx or 5xx as a transport failure and keeps the last value within the silence bound, and CoAP does the same for any response that is not a 2.xx (#791); an open HTTP or CoAP breaker no longer suspends polls, since it probes every interval. Sensors always connect with a clean session, and a persistent session is refused at config load in every spelling. `aiomqtt` is in the runtime lock, so the MQTT perception adapters install from the release bundle; connecting one on a Pi is not yet observed.

- 2026-09-30: The authority key registry loader is implemented to `evidence-exchange/v2`; the registry does not ship yet, and disposition verification is not wired. The loader holds every member to a string and both hexadecimal members to lowercase, recomputes each `key_id` from its key, refuses a public key that is not a canonical Ed25519 point under RFC 8032 section 5.1.3 or is of small order (decided by curve arithmetic), refuses one key under two purposes and a purpose without exactly one `active` key, accepts and parses the disposition purpose (which verifies nothing while no disposition verifier is installed), and refuses the whole registry on one refused key. - 2026-09-29: A cached sensor is silent once its value outlives the silence bound. The generic MQTT and MQTT perception adapters and the HTTP and CoAP pollers record each value's monotonic arrival and refuse a read past two poll intervals (never under 200 ms), so the staleness watch fires and the safety registry never observes a value from them whose arrival at the receiver is older than the silence bound (2 x the sensor's poll interval, at least 200 ms); producer timestamps and wall-clock steps do not change the age, and a value within the bound is still served. The bound is the receiver-observed arrival age under the supported posture: QoS 0 subscriptions, and a broker that does not queue QoS 0 for an offline client. It is not a general MQTT freshness guarantee. With `mqtt.clean_session: false` and a broker that queues QoS 0, a message buffered while the runtime was offline is timed from its delivery; that boundary is uncovered and tracked separately, as is a value an OPC UA server or other upstream cache serves from its own store. The Victron, LoRaWAN and Zigbee adapters are excluded and keep their earlier read behaviour: their producers publish on change or are duty-cycled -- Venus OS republishes a steady value only on a keepalive the adapter does not send, LoRaWAN uplinks are commonly minutes apart, and a Zigbee device may report a steady value only hourly -- so silence between messages does not show that a value is stale, and the poll interval is not the producer's reporting cadence. Their cached value is served however old it is, and a retained replay is cached, until a keepalive or reporting guarantee bounds freshness per protocol. A retained MQTT message replayed on subscribe is not a reading for the bounded adapters. The refusal is not counted by the circuit breaker, so a source that resumes is read at once.
